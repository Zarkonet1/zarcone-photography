The scoreboard at Passaic County Tech read 35-0 when the clock ran out on October 30. That's the number that goes in the record book. It isn't what I'll remember from that night.

Bridgewater-Raritan came into the NJSIAA North 2, Group 5 playoffs as the No. 5 seed, defending the first sectional championship in program history. They went 7-2 in the regular season — a 20-0 shutout at Westfield, a 7-3 grinder against Ridge, and a 28-7 win on Senior Night to send the seniors off at home. Then they got on a bus to Wayne to play the No. 4 seed on its own field, and the season ended there.

## The Game They Played

It's easy to let a final score rewrite the whole night. It shouldn't. This team didn't stop running, blocking, or tackling at any point, and the photos from the first whistle to the last look the same.

## When the Clock Runs Out

Something happens in the minutes after a season ends that doesn't happen at any other point in the year. Nobody leaves right away. Helmets come off. Players drop to a knee wherever they're standing. The noise from the other sideline fades out, and for a few minutes it's just them.

![Panther players kneel and bow their heads on the field after the final whistle](/photos/brhs-football-2026-final-game-01.jpg)

![A Panther lineman sits on the field, staring out past the end zone](/photos/brhs-football-2026-final-game-06.jpg)

![Two Panther players kneel together on the sideline after the game](/photos/brhs-football-2026-final-game-07.jpg)

These aren't photos of a team that lost. They're photos of a team that cared. For a lot of these players — the seniors most of all — that was the last time they'd put on this uniform with these guys, and you can watch them realize it.

![Panther players gather together, several in tears, after the season-ending loss](/photos/brhs-football-2026-final-game-08.jpg)

## The Brotherhood

The coaches who spend a whole season correcting footwork and calling out missed assignments are the same ones who walk onto the field afterward and hold a kid while he cries. That's the part of high school football most people outside a program never see. It's also the part that explains why anyone signs up for summer lifts and two-a-days in the first place.

## Why I Keep Shooting After a Loss

The instinct after a game like this is to put the camera down and give people their space. I understand that instinct, and I respect the space. But I don't stop, and here's why.

Ten years from now, nobody in these photos is going to remember the 35-0. They're going to remember the guy kneeling next to them, the coach who walked over, and what it felt like to care that much about something. These pictures are the record of that. They belong to the players and their families as much as the championship photos from last November do.

When I shared this set on Instagram, the response made it clear I wasn't the only one who saw it that way.
