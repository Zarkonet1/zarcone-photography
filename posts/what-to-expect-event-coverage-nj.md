Most people booking event coverage for the first time have the same unspoken worry: *what if the photos don't actually capture what happened?* A golf outing raises money for a scholarship fund. A 5K walk honors a cause a family has spent years fighting for. A corporate event is the one afternoon a year when a company's clients, sponsors, and staff are all in the same place. There's no reshoot. Whatever happens, happens once.

This post walks through what full event coverage actually looks like, from the planning call to the delivered gallery.

## Before the Event

Coverage starts with a short conversation about what the day is actually for. Not just the schedule — the purpose. A scholarship golf outing and a corporate client appreciation event both involve people standing on grass holding drivers, but the photos that matter are different. For a charity outing, that usually means the honoree, the sponsor signage, the check presentation, and the groups who came out to support it. For a corporate event, it's usually leadership, clients, and the moments that will end up in next year's sponsorship deck.

We ask for a run of show if one exists — tee times, program schedule, award presentation, anything with a fixed moment attached to it — so coverage doesn't miss what can't be repeated.

## Coverage, Start to Finish

This is full coverage, not a few hours in the middle of the day. We're there for arrival and setup, through the event itself, to the final moments — the last group photo, the last handshake, the teardown. Outdoor charity events in particular tend to have their best moments at the edges: people arriving with signs, kids in team shirts, the exhausted-but-happy finish-line shot nobody planned for.

That balloon arch below is a good example. It wasn't on any shot list. It was the natural gathering point for every family at the event, so it became one — and the resulting images are the ones the organization actually used for next year's promotion.

![Family group photo under balloon arch at charity 5K walk, New Jersey](/photos/EVENT-Zarcone-Photography-201.jpg)

The same instinct applies to milestone and religious celebrations — baptisms, first communions, life events with a fixed liturgical moment that happens exactly once, on schedule, whether or not the photographer is ready.

## After the Event

Files are professionally edited — color, exposure, and cropping — and delivered through a private online gallery, typically within two to three weeks. Larger or multi-day events run up to six weeks, simply because there's more to edit. The gallery is yours to access permanently, with full-resolution downloads for print, web, and social at any size. No watermarks, no per-image licensing fees, no gallery that quietly expires.

For events that repeat annually — golf outings, walks, galas — we offer recurring rates, since returning organizations already know their run of show and we already know their venue.

Videography add-ons are available for events where a highlight reel matters as much as the photos — increasingly common for charity and corporate events that need a recap video for next year's sponsor outreach.
